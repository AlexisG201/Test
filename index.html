Inspect assumptions, formulas & real-world context

What is synthetic?

All numeric inputs, household and firm counts, example families and firms, the fiscal ceiling, and response coefficients. This exercise explores trade-offs. It does not predict actual Indonesian policy outcomes. Anyone may question these assumptions. Sharing what you know about real policy is voluntary and is your own view, not a national fact.

Starting annual budget
Revenue Rp2,500T; spending Rp3,000T, including Rp400T fuel support and existing interest. These are teaching numbers. “Starting package” applies taxes +50T, subsidy reduction 25%, assistance +60T, and borrowing 400T.
Budget relationships
Savings = 400 × subsidy reduction ÷ 100. Revenue = 2,500 + additional tax. Spending = 3,000 − savings + additional assistance. Deficit = spending − revenue. Financing balance = borrowing − deficit. Borrowing does not reduce the deficit. A fiscal rule caps new borrowing at 400T (2% of a synthetic GDP of 20,000T), so a deficit above 400T cannot be fully financed.
Fuel-price response
Fuel-price increase (%) = 0.4 × subsidy-reduction percentage. This is an invented coefficient, not an estimate.
Counts and tax incidence
The model has 70 million households, of which 20 million receive assistance, and 5 million firms. Of any extra tax, 60% falls on households and 40% on firms. Added tax cost per family per month = tax (Rp T) × 1012 × 60% ÷ 70 million ÷ 12. Added tax cost per firm per month = tax × 1012 × 40% ÷ 5 million ÷ 12. These counts and shares are invented.
Families
Each earns Rp4m/month, spends Rp3m on other costs and Rp0.4m on fuel: Rp600,000 remains initially. Added fuel cost = 400,000 × fuel-price increase ÷ 100. Recipient transfer = assistance × 1012 ÷ 20 million ÷ 12. Non-recipients get no transfer.
Small firm
Sales Rp100m/month, other costs Rp75m, fuel Rp10m: baseline profit Rp15m. Extra fuel cost = 10m × fuel-price increase ÷ 100. This one example firm stands for firms in general. No change in sales, wages or prices is modelled, and real firms differ.
Interest and missing mechanisms
Illustrative future interest = new borrowing × 7%; excluded from this round’s spending. No principal-repayment path, exchange-rate effect, wage response, eligibility change, macroeconomic forecast, or behavioral feedback. Assistance is immediate in the core model. The classroom discussion can challenge these assumptions.
